Privacy policy

Last updated: 17 September 2026 · Covers the Osmium Sound Companion app for Android, the Osmium Sound player and this website

The short version. Osmium Sound has no user accounts and no advertising. The Android app collects nothing about you: it talks to the player you own, on your own network. Your music, your library and your settings stay on the player and on your phone. This website sets no cookies and keeps no record of your visit: your address is read to tell people from scanners and then dropped, and what stays behind is a count per day.

1. Who is responsible

Adriano Frongillo, sole developer of Osmium Sound, Italy — the data controller for the purposes of the GDPR.

  • Privacy questions: info@osmiumsound.it
  • Help with the product: support@osmiumsound.it

2. The Android app (Osmium Sound Companion)

The app is a remote control for a player you own. It sends nothing to us. There is no sign-up, no profile, no advertising, and no analytics or crash-reporting kit inside it.

  • Where your data is. The address of your player, your preferences and the players you have used are saved on the phone. Uninstalling the app removes them.
  • Who it talks to. Only the Osmium Sound player you point it at, over your home network — or over the remote connection you set up yourself. Track details and cover art come from that same player.
  • Downloads. A track you ask the app to download is saved on your phone and stays there.
  • Scrobbling. Off unless you switch it on. When it is on, the app hands the artist, album and track being played to a scrobbling app installed on the same phone; what that app does with it is covered by its own policy.

3. The Osmium Sound player

  • Your music stays home. Files, the library index, cover art and settings live on the player, on your network. We have no access to them.
  • Streaming services. If you connect a music service, that login is stored on the player and the traffic goes straight from the player to the service. What the service collects is described in its own policy.
  • Update checks. To see whether a new version exists, the player asks this website, and falls back to our download server (file.osmiumsound.it) and to GitHub if it cannot reach it. Nothing about how you use the player is sent. We do count those checks, to know roughly how many players are out there: the address and the name of the program become a short code, made with a secret that changes every week and is then thrown away, and the code goes into a counting summary and is gone. Nothing is written to your player, and nothing kept can be traced back to it.
  • Usage reports to the Lyrion project — the music server that runs inside the player — are switched off, and the setup wizard never turns them on unless you tick that box yourself.
  • Listening from outside the house happens only if you set it up, through the service you choose. We are not in the middle of that connection.
  • Support files. The player can save a file with logs and settings to help us with a problem. It is created only when you ask for it, and it reaches us only if you send it. You can open it and see what is inside before you do.

4. This website

What passes through, and what stays

Opening a page here sends your IP address and your browser's description to the server, as every request on the web does. They are used for one thing while the request lasts — telling a person from the scanners that go through any public site — and then they go with it. Neither one is written down. There is no visit log on this site: nothing kept here says that you were on it.

What stays is counts, day by day:

  • how many times each page was opened, and by how many different people;
  • how many times each file was downloaded — from here and from our download server — and by how many different people;
  • how many of those openings came from each country, browser, operating system and site you arrived from;
  • how much traffic was left out of the counts, for what reason, and from which network;
  • how many different Osmium Sound players asked whether there is a new version, week by week.

How “how many different people” is counted without keeping anyone

Your address and your browser's description, mixed with a secret that changes every week, become a short code. The code goes into that week's summary and is then gone. The summary does not hold the codes: it holds just enough trace of them to say how many different ones went in. It cannot be read backwards, it cannot be matched to you, and it cannot be lined up against another week's. The secret is deleted as soon as its week ends, so from that moment nobody — us included — can tell that two visits came from the same place.

A week, and not a day, because a secret that turned over at midnight made every count a count of days: the same person on Tuesday was a different code from the same person on Monday, so “how many different people” could only ever be answered one day at a time. The price is that two visits from the same address can be recognised as the same for seven days instead of one, inside the request and nowhere else — what is kept is the same summary as before, which holds nobody.

Networks are noted by name and number — the number that identifies an internet provider or a hosting company, never a person. It is the only way to notice that a whole provider has been left out of the counts by mistake.

Why, and on what basis

To know whether the project interests anyone and which pages are worth improving. That is a legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: your address and your browser's description are handled only while the request lasts and are never stored, and what stays behind counts pages, not people. No cookies — nothing is stored in your browser. No advertising, no profiling, nothing shared or sold.

Who can see it, and for how long

Only the data controller, through a password-protected dashboard. The counts sit in Cloudflare D1, Cloudflare's database service: for this, Cloudflare is a processor under Art. 28 GDPR and acts on our instructions. Cloudflare also delivers this site, and as the operator of that network it keeps logs of its own, addresses included, for its own purposes and its own periods — that part is its doing, not ours, and it is described in its privacy policy.

The daily counts stay while the site exists: there is nothing in them left to expire. The daily secret lasts one day. Nothing else is kept.

Staying out of the count

Use a browser with JavaScript turned off, or open the browser console on this site and run localStorage.setItem('osmium_ignore', '1').

Other parties involved in this site:

  • The pages are served by Cloudflare Pages and the downloads come from GitHub and from file.osmiumsound.it; like any web server, these see the request and your address. Our download server counts the file and forgets the address, exactly as the pages do; what GitHub and Cloudflare keep is theirs.
  • The page font is loaded from Google Fonts, which sees the request your browser makes to it.
  • The review form on the home page is by Tally and loads only when you open it. Links to GitHub, Ko-fi and Facebook lead to those sites, each with its own policy.

Where this goes in the world. Cloudflare, GitHub and Google are companies in the United States, so a request that reaches them — which carries your address, as every request does — leaves the European Union. Those transfers rest on the adequacy decision the European Commission adopted for the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, and, where that does not cover them, on the standard contractual clauses in the data processing terms each of them applies. We send them nothing on our own account: what they see is the request your browser makes.

5. What we never do

No advertising, no profiling, no selling or sharing of data, no third-party tracking kits in the app or in the player.

6. Children

Osmium Sound is not aimed at children, and nothing is knowingly collected about them.

7. Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what data we hold about you, get a copy, have it corrected or deleted, and object to how it is used. Write to info@osmiumsound.it.

One honest note about what this means here. For the app, the player and the website, the answer is almost always that we hold nothing about you: the app and the player never send us anything, and the site and the download server keep only counts, in which there is nothing of yours to find, correct or delete — not because we will not look, but because there is no line with your name, your address or your device on it. If that answer ever stops being true, this page changes with it.

If you think your data has been mishandled you can also complain to the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.

8. Changes to this page

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and anything that matters to you will be said plainly here.

4. Questo sito

Cosa passa e cosa resta

Aprire una pagina qui manda al server il tuo indirizzo IP e la descrizione del tuo browser, come fa qualsiasi richiesta sul web. Servono a una cosa sola, per il tempo della richiesta — distinguere una persona dagli scanner che passano su qualsiasi sito pubblico — e poi se ne vanno con lei. Né l'uno né l'altra vengono scritti da qualche parte. Su questo sito non c'è un registro delle visite: niente di quello che resta qui dice che ci sei stato tu.

Quello che resta sono conteggi, giorno per giorno:

  • quante volte è stata aperta ogni pagina, e da quante persone diverse;
  • quante volte è stato scaricato ogni file — da qui e dal nostro server dei download — e da quante persone diverse;
  • quante di quelle aperture sono arrivate da ogni Paese, browser, sistema operativo e sito di provenienza;
  • quanto traffico è stato lasciato fuori dai conteggi, per quale motivo e da quale rete;
  • quanti lettori Osmium Sound diversi hanno chiesto se c'è una versione nuova, settimana per settimana.

Come si contano “quante persone diverse” senza tenere nessuno

Il tuo indirizzo e la descrizione del tuo browser, mescolati con un segreto che cambia ogni settimana, diventano un codice breve. Il codice entra nel riassunto di quella settimana e poi sparisce. Il riassunto non contiene i codici: ne contiene giusto la traccia che serve per dire quanti ne sono entrati di diversi. Non si può leggere a ritroso, non si può ricondurre a te e non si può confrontare con quello di un'altra settimana. Il segreto viene cancellato appena la sua settimana finisce, quindi da quel momento nessuno — noi compresi — può sapere che due visite vengono dallo stesso posto.

Una settimana e non un giorno perché un segreto che cambiava a mezzanotte rendeva ogni conteggio un conteggio di giornate: la stessa persona martedì era un codice diverso dalla stessa persona lunedì, quindi “quante persone diverse” si poteva rispondere solo un giorno alla volta. Il prezzo è che due visite dallo stesso indirizzo si riconoscono come la stessa per sette giorni invece che per uno, dentro la richiesta e da nessun'altra parte — quello che resta conservato è lo stesso riassunto di prima, che non contiene nessuno.

Le reti vengono annotate per nome e numero — il numero che identifica un operatore internet o una società di hosting, mai una persona. È l'unico modo per accorgersi di aver lasciato fuori dai conteggi un intero operatore per sbaglio.
